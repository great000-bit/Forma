## Patterns

- **Case structure:** context, research question, why this method, participants and recruitment, what we did, key insights (with quotes), what changed because of it, limits, reflection.
- **Artifacts:** affinity maps, journey maps, quotes, charts, repository screenshots. Real photos of the process beat polished mock-ups.
- **Impact:** decisions changed, roadmap shifted, metrics moved, team adoption. Honest about small samples.
- **Gating:** many researchers lock detailed cases; a one-paragraph public summary plus "ask me" works.
- **Career story:** researchers often come from psychology, academia, journalism; the path is a strength.

## Required sections for the interview agent (UX Research)

1. Identity: name, title, level, city, remote.
2. Focus: methods you're strongest in (interviews, usability, surveys, diary studies, analytics, mixed methods) and domains.
3. Background: education or previous field (psychology, academia, etc.).
4. Studies (2 to 4): research question, why that method, participants (how many, how recruited), what you found (top 3 insights, quotes allowed if anonymised), what changed as a result, limits, artifacts (images), NDA yes/no.
5. Tools: Dovetail, Maze, UserTesting, Mixpanel, Figma, etc.
6. Experience: companies, roles, years.
7. Extras (optional): publications, talks, teaching, repository or ops work.
8. Contact: email, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, CV.

**Page sections:** hero, studies, approach/methods, about and background, experience, contact. **Niche-specific:** insight cards with quotes, "what changed" block per study, methods matrix, optional publications.
